01 / PURPOSE

Objectives of the trip

The trip was organized to explore the cultural heritage, natural landscapes, local practices, and distinctive communities of Northern Luzon. It also provided an opportunity to document observations, practice responsible tourism, and relate experiences to classroom learning.

  • Observe historical landmarks and local architecture.
  • Appreciate the region's natural environment and cultural traditions.
  • Record key activities, practical considerations, and lessons learned.
02 / ITINERARY

Schedule of activities

DayDestinationActivities and observations

Day 2Sagada, Mountain ProvinceVisit a mountain viewpoint, explore walking trails, and observe the highland environment and community life.
Day 3Banaue, IfugaoView the rice terraces, learn about the landscape's agricultural significance, and document local perspectives.

03 / OBSERVATIONS

Highlights and findings

Each destination offered a different perspective on the region. Vigan highlighted the value of preserving historic streets while supporting everyday community activity. Sagada provided opportunities to appreciate the mountain environment, while Banaue demonstrated the relationship between landscape, agriculture, and generations of local knowledge. Baguio presented a more urban setting where markets, creative spaces, and tourism intersect.

Travel time, weather, and road conditions influenced the pace of activities. Flexible scheduling made it easier to adjust plans, take breaks, and spend more time at locations that offered meaningful learning opportunities.

05 / CONCLUSION

Recommendations and conclusion

The trip emphasized the importance of preparation, time management, cultural awareness, and responsible travel. Future trips should allow for weather-related delays, confirm transportation and accommodation details in advance, and set aside time for rest and documentation.

Overall, the journey offered a broad view of Northern Luzon's cultural and environmental diversity. The combination of historical sites, highland landscapes, local food, and community spaces made the itinerary both educational and memorable.

THE MOMENT

Not every destination needs a schedule.

On the third morning, the plan was to leave before sunrise. Instead, rain arrived. We waited. Breakfast stretched into conversation, conversation stretched into an hour, and the hour became the best part of the day.
